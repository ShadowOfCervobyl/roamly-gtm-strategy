## 4. The assumption I'm pushing back on

> **AI's point (2): the assumption you're too close to see.** That the group is your customer. It isn't. The organiser is. One person plans, chases, fronts the money and carries the stress. Everyone else is a passenger who just wants a link and a price.

**My pushback:** The pishback is that okay, the person who organizes it, IS the customer but only in a way that he organizes the people, pays for the experience but the decision to go, and pick the experience and pick the guides is on the whole group not on an individual. It might be on an individual once he/she takes the organization of the trip, however, my assumption is that people need to agree in the group, where to go, if the price is suitable for the experience, etc. So the end-customer IS the group.

## 5. V1 GTM strategy one-pager

**V1 one-pager, short version:**

- **Customer:** friend and family groups bigger than a small party. The group decides, and the organiser is the way in.
- **Value proposition:** Plan it together, agree in one place, and everyone pays their own share.
- **Motion:** product-led only.
- **Channels (no paid marketing in V1):**
  - Offer Groups inside the product at the drop-off moment, for example when someone picks more seats than a small party, or when several accounts start booking the same slot.
  - A shareable group link for WhatsApp or iMessage. Invitees see the plan and their share before they sign up. This link is what brings in new users.
  - Email and push to users who already used the separate-accounts workaround.
  - A "group-friendly" badge on experiences whose hosts agree to take groups.
  - Support redirects group-booking tickets to Groups.
- **Success metric:** new users who pay their share through a group invite.
  - It only counts when the whole group completes the booking, so group booking completion rate is tracked alongside it.
  - Guardrails: individual bookings, host ratings and support volume must not get worse.
