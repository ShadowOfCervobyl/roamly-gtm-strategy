Roamly Groups: V1 GTM Strategy

Groups already try to book together on Roamly and drop off. We will turn those groups into completed bookings, and every invited member into a new Roamly user.

The problem

Groups book around the product, then give up

Roamly is built for individuals and small parties. Groups book the same experience across separate accounts, split payments by hand and sync availability outside the product. Many drop off. We lose bookings from people who already want to buy.

Signals

The demand is already in our data

  • High drop-off on group booking sessions
  • Repeated support reports of multi-account bookings and manual payment splits
  • Consistent feedback: users want to do this on Roamly

To validate: share of sessions with multi-account behaviour, typical group size, group vs. individual drop-off.

Who we serve

Friend and family groups bigger than a small party

The group decidesWhere to go, whether the price is worth it, which host.
The organiser brings it inStarts planning and shares the link. Our way in.

Later: corporate teams. They pay centrally and need a sales motion.

Value proposition

What we promise

Plan it together, agree in one place, and everyone pays their own share. One booking for the whole group, without the chasing.

How a group books

From "let's do something" to booked, inside Roamly

  1. Organiser starts

    Picks an experience or a shortlist and creates a group.

  2. Group joins

    One link in the group chat. Members see the plan and their share before signing up.

  3. Group agrees

    Compares options and price per person, then confirms in one place.

  4. Everyone pays

    Each member pays their own share. The booking confirms when all have paid.

Motion

Product-led, no sales team

  • Demand happens inside the product, so Groups appears at the moment groups drop off.
  • Every booking spreads the product: invitees use Roamly before they sign up.
  • A consumer price point doesn't need sales in V1.
Primary goal

Acquire new users through groups

New Roamly users who arrive through a group invite. It only happens when the group completes its booking, so completion is the gate.

Channels

Where Groups shows up

  • In-productOffer Groups when someone picks more seats than a small party, or when several accounts book the same slot.
  • Group linkOrganiser shares one link to WhatsApp or iMessage. This is the acquisition loop.
  • LifecycleEmail and push to users who already used the multi-account workaround.
  • Hosts"Group-friendly" badge on experiences whose hosts opt in.
  • SupportGroup-booking tickets are redirected to Groups.

No paid acquisition in V1.

How we measure

Success metric

MainNew users per month who pay their share through a group invite
GateGroup booking completion rate: groups started that end with everyone paid
SupportingTime from first invite to fully paid · Multi-account bookings going down
GuardrailsIndividual booking conversion · Host ratings · Support volume

Targets set after the baseline is measured.

Rollout

Prove it small, then scale

  1. Measure the baseline
  2. Recruit hosts who opt in to groups
  3. Beta with existing users in a few cities
  4. Fix payment and agreement failures
  5. Full launch
Risks and open questions

What could break it

  • Groups don't agree, or don't all pay. Fixing payment alone won't remove drop-off at the "do we all agree?" stage.
  • Host capacity. Can an experience built for 6 take 14, and at what price?
  • Group size threshold. Where does a "small party" end? To be set from booking data.
