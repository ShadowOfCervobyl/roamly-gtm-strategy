## 5. One-page recommendation

### Pricing Recommendation: Roamly Groups
*V1 draft · For Finance, Sales Leadership and Product Marketing*

#### The recommendation in one line
Each group member pays their own share plus a small transaction fee, shown upfront in one all-in price per person. Three tiers, from a standard group booking up to customised celebrations and teams. Most groups should pick the middle tier: a private experience.

#### Pricing goal: grow revenue from a known audience
- **Who first:** existing Roamly travellers who already try to book as groups across separate accounts. The demand is in our support data and drop-off rates.
- **Why this goal:** we earn revenue from bookings we are losing today, before spending on new markets. That's consistent with our bet: win on the group decision, and put new cities on hold.
- **Acquisition is a side effect, not the goal.** Every invitee who pays becomes a Roamly user.

#### Pricing model: transaction-based
- **Who pays:** every group member pays their own share of the experience, plus a transaction fee per person.
- **For what:** coordinating the group in one place. That means the shared plan, agreeing before booking, and nobody fronting the money.
- **How it's shown:** the fee is included in the price per person on the shared plan. There is no surprise at checkout.
  - That keeps our messaging promise to invitees: "know what it will cost you".
  - It also avoids the comparison with Airbnb, which shows guests no service fee.

#### Packaging
| Tier | Who it's for | Rough price |
|---|---|---|
| **Group** (entry) | Friends or families joining a standard experience together | Standard price per person + transaction fee |
| **Private Group** ⭐ *most should pick* | Groups who want the experience to themselves, with a shared plan and agreement tools | Private price set by the host + transaction fee |
| **Celebrations & Teams** (top) | Birthdays, reunions and team events that need a customised host experience, flexibility and invoicing | Custom quote from the host + transaction fee, invoicing available |

*Fee level and tier prices are hypotheses until tested. The brief and our research give no Roamly price data, so no figure here should be quoted as final.*

#### What each stakeholder needs to hear

**Finance**
- **Where the money comes from:** a transaction fee on each person's share, on bookings that drop off today. That's new revenue on existing demand, not spending on new markets.
- **The main financial risk is payment failure, not price.** If one member doesn't pay, we need a clear rule. Options:
  - hold the booking for a fixed window, then release the unpaid seats
  - let the organiser cover the gap, by choice and never by default
  - cancel and refund everyone

  The choice affects refunds, host payouts and how many bookings fail.
- **To decide before launch:** the fee level, the length of the payment window, and when hosts get paid if a group pays in parts.

**Sales Leadership**
- **All three tiers are self-serve in V1.** No tier needs a sales team, which matches our product-led plan.
- **Celebrations & Teams is the bridge to corporate teams, our next segment.** In V1 it stays self-serve, with invoicing and custom quotes from the host. A sales-assisted motion for corporate teams comes in a later phase.
- **Hosts are the partnership job in V1:** private and custom tiers only work with hosts who opt in to groups, so recruiting hosts for groups is where Sales Leadership can help now.

**Product Marketing**
- **Lead with the outcome, not the fee:** "One price per person. Everyone pays their own share." The fee is part of that price, not a separate line.
- **Push the middle tier:** a private experience is the clearest step up from Airbnb Experiences, where one person books a shared activity. It shows off what we're best at: the group decision plus a vetted host.
- **Know the comparison:** Airbnb charges guests no visible fee and its split payment is free (for stays). Our story has to be the agreement and the host quality, never "pay for split payment".

#### The assumption to test
**Everyone in the group actually pays their share in time.**
If they don't, bookings fail or the organiser ends up paying anyway. The core promise, "no one fronts the money, no one chases", collapses, and so does the per-member fee model.

#### Best research method: Conjoint, with one caveat
- **Conjoint** fits best of the three. It shows respondents complete offers that combine payment window, fee level, private vs standard, and what happens if someone doesn't pay, and it measures which trade-offs groups accept. That tells us which payment rules make on-time payment most likely, and how much fee they tolerate alongside those rules.
- **Van Westendorp** measures only acceptable price ranges. It's useful later to set the fee level, but it says nothing about whether people pay on time.
- **Max-Diff** ranks how much features matter. It could show that "nobody fronts the money" matters most, but not whether people behave that way.
- **Caveat:** all three measure what people say. Whether they actually pay on time is behaviour, so confirm the Conjoint result in the beta with existing users (Module 1 rollout) by tracking the share of groups where every member pays within the window.
