What I built

Five modules, one go-to-market plan

Each module added one decision. Together they set who Roamly Groups is for, how it wins and how it makes money.

Module 1
GTM strategy
Target segment

Independent travellers that would like to travel with friends, or family, or partners. Since these segments would like to share memories, and experience this is the most valuable user segment to target. (Refined after the pressure test: friend and family groups bigger than a small party. The group decides, and the organiser brings it to Roamly.)

GTM motion

PLG: Product-led since this is a new feature for an existing product, and we would like to first drive the acquisition of the new feature for the users. Based on the logic of the product I have chose only PLG as the strategy not the combination.

Module 2
Battlecard and strategic bet
Primary competitor

Airbnb Experiences

Strategic bet

I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by building for the group decision, not just payments.

Deprioritizing

Module 3
Positioning
Target audience

Groups of friends and families, bigger than a small party, who plan a shared experience together. The group decides, and one organiser brings the group to Roamly.

Positioning statement

For friend and family groups planning a shared experience in one of Roamly's 40 cities, who want to agree on something they will all enjoy without one person paying for everyone and chasing the money, Roamly Groups is the group booking feature that takes the whole group from "let's do something" to booked: everyone sees the plan and their price, agrees in one place, and pays their own share. Unlike Airbnb Experiences, where one person books an activity alongside a stay, Roamly Groups is built around the group's decision and delivered by 12,000+ vetted local hosts whose travellers come back three times more often than the industry average.

Module 4
Messaging pillars
From/To shift

From: Friends and families want to share an experience, but choosing one everyone likes takes coordination. They often book through a product built around one person’s decision, and that person may end up paying upfront and chasing everyone else. To: A group can agree on an experience and its price before booking. Everyone sees the same plan, has a say, and pays their own share, so the organiser can bring the group together without carrying the decision or the bill alone.

Audience 1 pillar

For: The organiser bringing friends or family together to plan an experience in one of Roamly’s 40 cities.
- Value prop: Get the group from “let’s do something” to a booked experience everyone has agreed on, without having to make the choice alone or chase people for money.
- Capabilities: Share the plan and price per person in one place, let everyone agree before booking, and have each person pay their own share.
- Evidence: Roamly works with 12,000+ vetted local hosts. Their travellers book again at three times the industry average. To prove in beta: group booking completion rate.

Copy across three surfaces

Surface 1: Homepage headline: "One experience the whole group agrees on. No money chasing after." Leads with the value prop: connects the shared decision directly to the organiser’s relief.
Surface 2: Email subject line: "Can everyone agree on the plan before you book?" Leads with the value prop: names the organiser’s immediate challenge and frames the email around resolving it.
Surface 3: Onboarding tooltip: "Invite everyone to review the plan and their price. You won’t need to cover the group’s bill yourself." Leads with the value prop: reassures the organiser about the outcome while tying it to the group’s individual prices.

Module 5
Pricing recommendation
Goal and model

Goal: Grow revenue from a known audience. This refines my Module 1 goal ("Acquire"): the battlecard showed that earning revenue from group bookings Roamly already loses is a safer first win than chasing new users against Airbnb. New users from group invites remain a side effect.
Model: Transaction-based
Who pays: Each group member pays their share plus a transaction fee, shown together as one all-in price per person.

Tiers

Entry (Group): group booking with split payment on a standard experience
Middle (Private Group, the one most should pick): private experience for the group, with a shared plan and agreement tools
Top (Celebrations & Teams): celebrations or teams, with a customised host experience, flexibility and invoicing

Assumption to test

Everyone in the group actually pays their share in time. If not, bookings fail, or the organiser ends up paying anyway, and the core promise collapses.

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

The course was very interesting, and I enjoyed it very much. The biggest friction was fully understanding the concepts we were taught and then applying them in each lab.

This is not my first Product School course, so my understanding of PM changes after every course. I am more and more interested in what PMs are responsible for, which, mostly in smaller corporations or companies, is either not properly understood or not communicated.
